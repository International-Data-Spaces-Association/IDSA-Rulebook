# IDSA Rulebook — International Perspectives

A subgroup of the IDSA Rulebook Working Group

## Why this exists

IDSA is a truly global organisation, but the data space movement is not evolving uniformly. Standards converge through formal bodies: ISO/IEC 20151, the Dataspace Protocol, CEN/CENELEC JTC 25. What diverges is everything underneath: governance philosophy, legal regimes, market structures, and geopolitical positioning.
The IDSA Rulebook was developed primarily in and for the European context. Several of its assumptions - harmonised horizontal legislation, institutional trust anchored in public bodies, federated multi-stakeholder consortia as the default market structure - are European characteristics, not universal ones. For the Rulebook to serve a global community, those assumptions must be made explicit, and the conditions in other regions must be described by the people who work in them.

That is what this subgroup produces: **regional annexes to the IDSA Rulebook**, each describing how data spaces actually work (legally, institutionally, commercially) in one region of the world.

## The annexes

Each annex follows a common nine-section structure so that regions can be compared side by side:

1. Overview
2. Key Initiatives and Infrastructure
3. Legal and Regulatory Dimension
4. Data Sovereignty and Governance
5. Interoperability in Data Spaces
6. Trust, Privacy, and Security
7. Public-Private Collaboration
8. Cross-Border Data Flows
9. Open Questions for Future Research

## How to contribute

**Comment on a published annex.** Open a GitHub issue or submit a pull request. Every annex is a living document: regulatory content is date-stamped and updated as the landscape moves, and the open questions in Section 9 are tracked as issues in this repository.

**Lead or join a regional annex.** You do not need to be a lawyer or a standards expert. The most valuable contributors are practitioners who know how data sharing actually works in their region: which initiatives matter, which rules bind, where the friction is. The nine-section template and the published Europe annex give you the structure; the Working Group provides editorial support and review. A first draft of an annex is typically a small-team effort over a few months.

**Connect us to the right people.** If you know an organisation, hub, or colleague who should be involved in a regional annex, make the introduction.

## Working principles

- **Descriptive, not evaluative.** Annexes describe how each region works, in that region's own terms. They do not rank approaches.
- **Written by the region, reviewed by the Working Group.** Regional teams hold the pen; the WG ensures comparability and editorial quality.
- **Living documents.** Regulatory landscapes move. Annexes carry a status date, and updates are normal, expected, and welcome.
- **Analysis, not policy.** Annexes are Working Group analysis and do not constitute formal IDSA policy positions.
